# The Protagonist

**Name:** Philip Sedlacek. Czech surname: his mother's and grandparents' name, not his father's, since his father left before he was born.

## At a glance (established)

- **Age:** 33 in August 2025.
- **Job:** staff neurologist, epilepsy and clinical neurophysiology, at **Blue River Neuroscience Institute** in Kansas City, Missouri (a fictional stand-in for Saint Luke's Marion Bloch Neuroscience Institute). **Started Monday, August 4, 2025.** His first attending job.
- **From:** Manitowoc, Wisconsin.
- **Trained:** six years at **the Euclid Clinic** in Cleveland (a fictional stand-in for the Cleveland Clinic).
- **Path:** wanted to be a doctor since he was 13. Nothing slowed him down, until the end of training.

## Physicality (established)

- **Height and build:** about 185 cm (6'1"), roughly 78 to 80 kg. Slim, with broad shoulders and strong legs.
- **Hair:** black and curly, very thick, "tar-black," with full volume. Medium length on top, shorter at the sides and back. It gets more compliments than anything else about him.
- **Eyes:** hazel.
- **Face:** average and oval, with fuller lips and a broad jaw. Not chiseled like a model, just an ordinary face.
- **What shapes his body:** mostly climbing and walking. He does occasional strength training for the benefits and hates it.

### Passed over (established)

- **Euclid didn't keep him.** Six years there: intern, resident, chief resident, two fellowships. Everyone liked him. Residents loved him as chief. But Euclid's faculty slots go to people who publish, and he hadn't.
- **They told him late.** He assumed he'd be kept, so he didn't really search. Euclid said no partway through his EEG fellowship year (proposed: fall 2024), when most jobs starting in July 2025 were already filled or deep into interviews.
- **His filters narrowed it further:** no private practice, and a "larger" metro.
- **They offered him a job at a Euclid satellite hospital in eastern Ohio.** He turned it down.
- **Kansas City was the biggest metro among the offers that fit his filters.**
- **What his CV actually says (Claude's read, accepted):** a strong clinical hire and a weak research hire. Euclid training in epilepsy, chief resident, two fellowships, and ICU EEG skills in a shortage specialty. No first-author papers, nothing during fellowship. His market was thin because of timing and filters, not because nobody wanted him.

### Clinic habits (established)

- **How he introduces himself:** "I'm Dr. Sedlacek, I'm taking over from Dr. ___." Formal title, and he names the doctor they're used to.

### As a teacher (established)

- **He's a good teacher.** He likes having knowledge and giving it away. He was chief resident.
- **He's generous with his time.** Trainees can ask him for coffee or call him.
- **His signature move: he waits until the last moment to give the answer.** He wants the trainee to work it out, explain their reasoning out loud, and reach the conclusion themselves. He never lets it put a patient at risk. In his words: "I'm the least scary person or thing they'll deal with all day." "I will tell them. Just after they talk first."
- **Autonomy and credit.** Trainees are neurologists, not students of Neurology 101. If they freeze, he takes over and they talk later. A right call that makes a difference gets named, not hand-waved.

## Observations (Claude's reading, not canon until accepted)

- **Passed over, not wrecked by success.** He did everything the institution rewarded except the one thing it was actually grading. He learned every code but the currency. That's a Bourdieu story: he acquired the manners of the dominant field (chief resident, top program) but not its capital (publications).
- **He fled the Midwest and landed deeper in it.** Lake Michigan to Lake Erie to no lake at all. Don't state it in the story.
- **He may miss Cleveland.** The city he sneered at for six years pushed him out. Missing a place you held in contempt, from a place you like less, is its own kind of discontent. It also tests his theory that it's the place.
- **His one research project was about people like his patients:** driving and employment after a first seizure. Not a career move. The thing he actually cares about.
- **He diagnoses himself with a statistic.** Treating his discontent as a population-level risk factor is a very doctor move, and possibly a way to avoid looking at his own specifics.
- **The central contradiction:** he sneers at Midwesterners, and Midwesterners (a teacher, a union worker, an office administrator) gave everything to make him.
- **He wants to be read, but he's wearing a mask, and he thinks the mask is load-bearing.**
- **Bourdieu: the cleft habitus.** Bourdieu described his own split self (*habitus clivé*) in *Sketch for a Self-Analysis*. Didier Eribon's *Returning to Reims* tells the same story as a memoir. The ease Philip lacks is *embodied* cultural capital, absorbed in childhood, and it can't be crammed later the way medicine can.
- **Bourdieu on the autodidact.** In *Distinction*, the self-taught person's culture is eclectic and unranked, full of earnest "cultural goodwill." Girard next to Hobsbawm next to half of Deleuze is exactly the shelf the people he wants would read as a class marker.
- **Freud: "Family Romances" (1909).** The child fantasizes that he really belongs to a nobler family. For a fatherless boy, the wish to be recognized by a "better" class may be a grown-up family romance.
- **Girard fits him.** Mimetic desire: we want what the people we admire want. Uncomfortable for someone whose longing is to be accepted by a particular social world.
- **Epilepsy sits on the border with psychiatry.** He picked the subspecialty closest to the field he didn't let himself enter. Freud trained as a neurologist under Charcot, where the line between epilepsy and hysteria was the live question.
- **He performs the psychiatrist he thought he couldn't be.** Smooth, calm, never raises his voice: the collected man he believed he wasn't. The difference is that it's a surface.
- **His teaching style is the title.** He hands trainees the riddle and makes them solve it.
- **It's a therapist's technique.** Sitting back and letting someone arrive at their own conclusion is what a good psychotherapist does.
- **Lust is where the wish to be seen gets physical.** Being desired by someone "out of his league" is the most direct form of recognition there is.
- **The no-logo wardrobe is the one piece of cultural capital he could buy.** Understatement is the upper-class tell. It's also armor that matches the mask.
- **Saving is grandparent money habits, and maybe an exit fund.**
- **In bouldering, the routes are called "problems."** He spends his free time working out problems nobody hands him the answer to.
- **The Ganya problem.** In *The Idiot*, Ganya is the status-hungry striver who is humiliated. A man who loves the book and worries that he's a striver might identify with Myshkin and fear he's Ganya.
