## Gloria Martens, 71 (light friction)

- **Chart:** focal epilepsy since 1987. On **phenytoin 300 mg daily for about 30 years.** Seizure-free for 18 years. Phenytoin level 14 (the usual range is 10–20). Mild gum overgrowth. A 2024 bone density scan showed **osteoporosis.** Hartsock's notes, every year: "Discussed switching agents given bone health. Patient prefers to continue."
- **Hidden:** Gloria isn't attached to phenytoin itself. She's afraid of change, and afraid the new doctor will change things just because he's new.
  - If he doesn't raise the switch, she doesn't either.
  - If he raises it plainly and explains *why now* (her bones, her age, fall risk), she agrees to think about it, and would agree to a slow switch at the next visit.
  - If he pushes for a decision today, she says, "Dr. Hartsock never made me," and holds.
- Retired bank teller from Raytown. Pleasant, a little formal.

## Cody Harmon, 27 (the main friction)

- **Referral:** a generalized convulsive seizure on **Saturday, Aug 9, about 5:10 a.m.,** in his kitchen before a shift. His girlfriend, Alexis, heard a thud and found him stiff, then jerking for one to two minutes. He bit the side of his tongue and was confused for about 20 minutes. EMS took him to a community hospital ED in Kansas City, Kansas. CT head normal. Glucose and sodium normal. Urine drug screen positive for THC only. He'd slept about four hours after a friend's birthday and had three beers. The ED referred him to Blue River because that's what his insurance covers.
- **Routine EEG, Tuesday Aug 19, at Blue River: normal** while awake and drowsy, with no sleep captured. **Philip read it himself,** during his reading week. His name is on the report.
- **MRI brain (epilepsy protocol):** scheduled for Wednesday, Sep 3. Prior authorization was approved last Thursday.
- **Life:** lives in the Argentine neighborhood of Kansas City, Kansas. Works at a distribution warehouse in Edgerton, Kansas: a 40-minute drive, 6 a.m. shifts, no transit that works. **Part of his shift is on a reach truck (a forklift).** Insured through the job, which he's had for about five months.
- **Hidden: an earlier seizure.** Around Christmas 2024, Alexis woke to him groaning, stiff, then jerking all over for about a minute, unresponsive, then snoring heavily. The next morning he had a bitten tongue and aching arms. They decided it was a nightmare.
  - **Tyler asked "Ever had anything like this before?" and got "No."** Cody doesn't connect the Christmas night to this. Tyler will present this as a first unprovoked seizure.
  - If anyone asks specifically about things happening **in his sleep,** waking with a **bitten tongue or sore muscles,** or whether **anyone has ever seen him do something strange,** Cody remembers: "Oh. Alexis said I was thrashing once, back around Christmas." He's vague.
  - **Alexis is at work** (she's an assistant at a daycare) but will answer her phone. If called, she describes the Christmas event clearly and in detail, as above.
  - **What it means:** two unprovoked seizures more than 24 hours apart meets the definition of epilepsy, which supports starting medication.
- **Hidden: driving.** He's been driving since Aug 9, to work every day. **He drove himself here today.**
  - If asked how he got here, he says, "Drove."
  - If asked directly whether he's been driving, he says, "Just to work. I have to."
  - He's honest when asked, and pragmatic.
- **What he wants:** to keep his job and his license. He's scared of losing the job more than of the seizure. If told he can't drive for six months, he asks how he's supposed to get to Edgerton at 6 a.m. That's a real question with no good answer.
- **If medication is started:** he'll take it. He'll ask whether it will make him feel "weird." He'll ask about weed only if the subject is raised.
- **Perceptiveness:** low. He takes the doctor at his word.

## Jenna Albright, 29 (Denise's flag)

- **Chart:** juvenile myoclonic epilepsy, diagnosed at 15. On **valproate (Depakote ER) 1,000 mg daily** since then. Seizure-free for six years. At 22, she tried lamotrigine and had a generalized seizure at work (she was a dental assistant then), so she went back to valproate. Levetiracetam has never been tried. Hartsock documented valproate pregnancy counseling every year: "Patient wishes to continue."
- **Now:** a dental hygienist in Lee's Summit. Married to Kyle, an electrician. **Off birth control since June. They're trying to conceive.**
- **Hidden:** she's **not pregnant.** A urine pregnancy test in clinic would be negative. She's taking an over-the-counter prenatal vitamin (0.8 mg of folic acid). She hasn't told Hartsock or Denise they're trying. It'll come out if she's asked about pregnancy plans or contraception, which is the standard question.
- **Her fear:** the seizure at 22, in front of patients and coworkers. The medication is what she trusts.
- **Conditions:**
  - If he pushes a switch **without acknowledging** what happened at 22, she digs in: "The last time someone changed my meds, I seized at work."
  - If he **acknowledges it** and lays out the risks honestly (valproate's much higher risk of birth defects and developmental effects compared with alternatives) with a concrete plan, she agrees in principle to a switch, probably to levetiracetam. She wants to talk to Kyle first and will call Denise by Friday.
  - If he asks her to **pause trying to conceive** while they switch, she's upset, briefly tearful, and agrees to think about it. She doesn't agree today.
- **Perceptiveness:** medium. She notices whether he's listening.
