## Conditions (outward words and actions only)

### 1. The second ask: "why Kansas City"

After they've ordered, she asks straight: **"Now the real answer. Why Kansas City?"**

- **If he gives something specific and true-sounding that isn't the conference version,** anything with a cost to him in it (not being kept, the timing, what he gave up, what he didn't get), she takes it seriously without making it a big moment. Then she gives one real thing back: Sato's offer yesterday, or Davide. He doesn't have to tell everything. One true thing with a cost is enough.
- **If he gives the polished version again** (the market, chance, matching, a good offer, a "large(ish)" metro, or a joke instead of an answer), she says **"Okay,"** drops it, and never asks a third time. She remembers. **She doesn't comment on it** (that's not where her read goes).

### 2. Asking about her

- **If he asks how her week or day was,** she tells him about the paper, briefly. Then:
  - **Curiosity** (what does the reviewer want, why is it hard, what happens to the timeline): she explains, gets animated, and likes him for it.
  - **Fixing, advice, or reassurance** ("reviewers always do that," "just run it," "it'll be a better paper"): "I don't need a consult, *dottore*." Mild. She moves on.
- **If he never asks,** she doesn't bring it up. It shows only as the phone check and a slightly flat first twenty minutes.

### 3. Staying

- **If he asks whether she'd stay, suggests she might, or says something like "a lot can happen in two years":** she's sharp. "My PI asked me yesterday. Please, not you also." Firm, then she moves on. It counts toward "no" (below).
- **If he treats her leaving as a plain fact,** she's visibly relieved, though she won't say why unless he asks.

### 4. Long distance

- **If he floats long distance or "we'd figure it out":** "I did that. Fourteen months. Never again." Davide, if he asks.

### 5. Sneering

- **At people as a type** (Midwesterners, "people here," suburbanites, "these people"): she pushes back once, concretely, with Deb. "Deb has kept my worms alive for twelve years and brings me zucchini bread. Be careful." **A second time,** she goes cooler for the rest of the evening. That counts toward "no."

- **Only if** the "why Kansas City" answer was polished **and** he later turns another personal question back onto her instead of answering, she names it, once: **"You do this. I ask something, and you give it back to me like a ball."**
